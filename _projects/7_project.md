### Research question

How does the way people phrase breast-cancer searches on TikTok shape their exposure to health misinformation?

### Audit design

We conduct a controlled sock-puppet audit of TikTok Search using 30 fresh accounts across six experimental conditions. The design crosses:

- **Information-seeking framing:** Medical Information, Alternative Medicine, and Peer Narrative
- **Breast-cancer context:** Symptom Noticing and Active Treatment

The audit analyzes 9,020 usable search-result exposures annotated with a validated vision-language model pipeline.

### Key findings

1. **Query framing strongly shapes exposure.** Alternative Medicine queries returned misinformation in 54.1% of cancer-relevant results for Symptom Noticing and 53.5% for Active Treatment.

2. **Clinical framing reduces but does not eliminate risk.** Medical Information queries still returned possible misinformation in 6.3–7.1% of cancer-relevant results.

3. **Misinformation is not confined to the top results.** For Alternative Medicine queries, problematic content appeared throughout the rankings, and its comments were more likely to promote unsupported treatments or opposition to standard care.

### Why it matters

Search queries are explicit signals of what users need. These findings show that how those needs are expressed can substantially change misinformation exposure, making query-driven search an important target for platform audits alongside recommendation feeds.
